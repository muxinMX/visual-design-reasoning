# Design Reasoning

Use this module for original concepts, characters, visual systems, worldbuilding, teams, variants, and scene ideas.

## Work from cause to form

Derive visible choices from the brief rather than decorating a generic solution:

1. purpose, identity, audience, context, and desired effect;
2. behavioral, narrative, functional, or brand logic;
3. shape language, silhouette, proportion, hierarchy, and system rules;
4. components, clothing, props, typography, environment, or interaction;
5. color, material, detail density, and production constraints;
6. recognition, differentiation, extensibility, and feasibility.

Use only the categories relevant to the design object. Do not force a product, environment, packaging system, or graphic layout into a character-design template.

## Exploration quality

When the user asks for directions, vary the governing idea, not only color or accessories. Each direction should include:

- a concise concept;
- the core visual logic;
- what visibly distinguishes it;
- strengths and limitations;
- extension or production implications;
- a recommendation when useful.

Keep unselected directions provisional.

## Common modes

### Character or subject design

Connect identity and behavior to silhouette, body or component proportions, face or focal features, clothing or casing, props, palette, material, and representative pose. Check recognition at silhouette and thumbnail scale.

### Series and variants

Define invariants shared by the family, then a limited set of variables that create identity. Evaluate both family resemblance and individual recognition. Avoid uncontrolled complexity growth.

### Worldbuilding

Develop the premise, rules, environments, culture or operating context, sources of conflict, everyday behavior, and visual vocabulary before locking specific character appearance. Compare long-term story and design potential.

### Teams and relationships

Use role, personality, scale, silhouette, color weight, behavior, and interaction to create contrast and cohesion. Relationships should generate visible situations, not remain biography-only.

### Scene ideation

Derive scenes from subject behavior, context, conflict, interaction, or function. Define the key moment, visual hook, spatial relationship, and why the scene suits the subject.

### Architecture and spatial design

Match the output scope to the user's stated design priorities. When the request emphasizes building proportion, spatial relationships, and material expression, develop those as the core solution. Add furniture, camera or lens choices, and technical lighting parameters only when they are requested or materially necessary to explain the architecture or spatial experience. Do not expand every available design dimension by default.

## Refinement

When a direction is selected, record its invariants and open questions. Improve weak areas without silently replacing the core concept. Pass composition, motion, or lighting problems to `composition-motion.md` and prompt requests to `prompt-generation.md`.
