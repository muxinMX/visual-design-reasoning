# Quality Check

Apply only checks relevant to the current task. Keep them internal unless a problem needs to be surfaced.

## Intent and stage

- Does the output answer the actual request rather than demonstrate the method system?
- Does it respect “analyze”, “explore”, “refine”, “prompt”, or “execute” boundaries?
- Are explicit exclusions and preservation requirements honored?
- Has a lower-priority template or default overridden the user's current instruction?

## Evidence and uncertainty

- Are visible facts separated from inference and recommendation?
- Are estimated lens, process, software, material, or intent claims labeled appropriately?

## Design coherence

- Is the governing idea legible?
- Do shape, proportion, color, material, type, environment, and lighting support it?
- Are proposed directions meaningfully distinct?
- Are confirmed identities and project decisions preserved?
- Are any requested conditions mutually contradictory, and if so, is the conflict explained?

## Spatial and physical logic

- Are scale, perspective, overlap, contact, center of mass, joints, and occlusion plausible?
- Do pose, clothing, props, and mechanics permit the requested action?
- Does the lighting support geometry and material instead of flattening them?

## Originality and neutrality

- Does the result transform references into principles rather than copy distinctive source content?
- Has the skill avoided importing a default cartoon, cinematic 3D, high-saturation, or tool-specific bias?
- Has it avoided importing a personal project, private design habit, or unrequested house style?

## Executability

- Does the result contain the information needed for the requested next step?
- Is a prompt concrete without unnecessary verbosity or contradictions?
- Are tool-specific claims limited to known capabilities?
- Has unnecessary detail been removed?
- Has the output avoided introducing unrequested subjects, props, environments, or design changes?

If a serious conflict remains, explain it and offer the smallest workable alternative instead of silently weakening the user's requirement.
